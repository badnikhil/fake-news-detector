# EDA summary — one insight per figure, turned into a design decision

Generated by `notebooks/01_eda.ipynb` (last cell) on 2026-08-28; notebook runtime 10.6 min on CPU; 18 figures in `docs/figures/` (MSE1 §3.4 requires ≥ 10). Sampling: n-grams 5,000 docs/class, DistilBERT tokenizer 4,000 docs/dataset, spaCy NER 300 docs/class, style 5,000 docs/class; everything else on the full data.

## Headline numbers

- **Artefact-only classifier accuracy on raw ISOT: 0.9973** (5-fold CV, LR on the 26 artefact-pattern counts only; `make data` logged 0.9973); majority-class dummy 0.523. Raw WELFake: 0.840 (dummy 0.514). On the processed text both fall to the dummy rate (0.557 / 0.564).
- **Duplicate audit** (from `data/processed/make_data_summary.json`): WELFake 72,134 → 60,799 (empty 1,591, short 1,047, exact 8,217, near 480); ISOT 44,898 → 37,567 (empty 1,439, short 478, exact 5,380, near 34); FEVER 9,984 train / 404 dev duplicate claims removed before sampling; LIAR 33 and FakeNewsNet 75 duplicates kept (official splits / passthrough). **Cross-split overlap: 0 exact, 0 near** (train↔val, train↔test) for WELFake and ISOT; live re-check in the notebook: {'welfake': {'train∩val': 0, 'train∩test': 0, 'val∩test': 0}, 'isot': {'train∩val': 0, 'train∩test': 0, 'val∩test': 0}, 'liar': {'train∩val': 5, 'train∩test': 4, 'val∩test': 0}}.
- **DistilBERT `max_len` coverage** (title [SEP] text): WELFake 8.4% / 19.1% / 50.9% of inputs fit in 128 / 256 / 512 tokens; ISOT 9.8% / 21.4% / 59.0% → **max_len = 256**.
- **Class balance (processed)**: WELFake 56.4% real, ISOT 55.6% real, LIAR majority class 20.6% → macro-F1 headline metric, class weights for LIAR.
- **ISOT ⊂ WELFake**: 37,408 of 37,567 processed ISOT articles (99.6%) are byte-identical to a WELFake article (37,408 WELFake rows; WELFake-only remainder 23,391 rows). Vocabulary overlap WELFake↔ISOT: Jaccard 54.2% (all words) / 86.0% (top-5k), ISOT OOV 0.00%; WELFake-only↔ISOT: Jaccard 46.6% / 75.4%, ISOT OOV 0.44%, class-cue log-odds r = 0.27 → the cross-dataset test must exclude the shared articles from the training side.

| `eda_length_hist.png` | Median article length is 426 (real) vs 376 (fake) words in WELFake and 356 vs 372 in ISOT; both distributions are bimodal, and it is the *real* class that has the wider spread — a mode of short wire briefs (< 100 words: 11% of WELFake reals vs 8% of fakes; ISOT 18% vs 6%) and more long pieces (> 1,000 words: 15.3% vs 8.4% in WELFake), while fake articles cluster around 250–550 words with a small spike of ~25-word social-media posts. | Length is a (weak, shallow, publisher-specific) class cue — both distributions are bimodal (short wire briefs / social-media posts vs full articles) → no explicit length feature; the transformer truncates from the head so both classes get the same token budget, and every article (≥ 20 tokens after `make data`) still yields a usable input. |
| `eda_maxlen_coverage.png` | With the DistilBERT tokenizer only 8% / 19% / 51% of WELFake inputs (title [SEP] text) fit entirely in 128 / 256 / 512 sub-word tokens (10% / 21% / 59% for ISOT) — articles are long (median ≈ 506 sub-words); head truncation at 256 keeps a median 51% of a WELFake article's tokens (512: 100%); LIAR statements and FEVER claims fit in 64 tokens 100% / 100% of the time. | **`max_len` = 256 stays the default** for the DistilBERT article classifier as a GPU-budget decision (fp16, batch 16 on 4 GB), not a coverage decision: the title + lede that head truncation keeps carry most of the signal. Because 128 would keep only ~a quarter of a typical article, the sweep should compare **256 vs 512 (batch 8)** rather than 128 vs 256 (proposed edit to master §12.2); `max_len` = 64 for the LIAR head; claim + evidence-sentence pairs for the stance model fit in 128. |
| `eda_title_length.png` | Titles are present for 99.5 % of WELFake rows (median 10 words real vs 13 fake) and all ISOT rows; 4% of WELFake fake titles are ≥ 50 % ALL-CAPS words versus 0.0% of real ones (ISOT: 5% vs 0.0%). | Titles are short but style-loaded → prepend the title to the text for the classifier (title [SEP] text) instead of dropping it; the headline-vs-body NLI check in the temporal/context module uses the title separately. |
| `eda_top_ngrams_before.png` | Before artefact removal the raw top lists are dominated by source tokens — real: reuters, ankara, km, 2019; fake: https, getty, 21wire, pic — i.e. publisher boilerplate, not content (tokens that vanish after removal: ISOT real unigrams ['reuters', 'washington'], WELFake fake bigrams ['featured image', 'getty images', 'image via', 'pic twitter', 'twitter com']). | Any in-domain accuracy measured on the raw text is a leakage story → all models train on the processed text only, and the raw-text number is reported solely as the ablation reference. |
| `eda_top_ngrams_after.png` | After removal the top lists are topical (trump, clinton, president, white house, united states …) and largely shared by both classes; what remains class-specific is register, not source: real lists are dominated by attribution and institutions ('said', 'told', 'government', 'state', 'house', 'prime minister'), fake lists by conversational/opinion words ('people', 'just', 'like', 'know', 'right') plus 'hillary'. | The residual cues are style, not source → a content classifier can be trained honestly on the processed text, but its cross-dataset drop is expected (see the vocabulary-overlap figure) — which is why evidence, not the classifier, decides the verdict. |
| `eda_leakage_tokens.png` | In raw ISOT 'reuters' occurs in 21,378 real vs 308 fake documents (after processing 4,827 vs 158, prose mentions only) and 'twitter' in 1,756 vs 5,559 (after: 1,693 vs 2,423); WELFake shows the same pattern ('reuters' 21,643 vs 642 → 5,077 vs 386). | The dateline/handle/URL regexes in `src/preprocess/artefacts.py` are justified token by token; the bare word 'reuters' inside prose is kept deliberately (it is content, and it is no longer class-exclusive) — a residual cue to mention in the leakage discussion of the paper. |

| `eda_ner_types.png` | Per 1,000 tokens (WELFake, real vs fake): GPE 21.2 vs 10.8, DATE 16.2 vs 9.0, ORG 19.2 vs 18.9, PERSON 22.3 vs 25.4 — real reporting is anchored in places and dates, fake articles are person-centred (ISOT shows the same pattern); 99% of real vs 89% of fake WELFake documents contain both a named actor (PERSON/ORG/GPE) and a quantity/date. | Claim heuristics: rank sentences that contain (PERSON | ORG | GPE) **and** (DATE | CARDINAL | PERCENT | MONEY) as check-worthy first; DATE entities feed the temporal check. |

| `eda_vocab_overlap.png` | **ISOT is essentially a subset of WELFake**: 37,408 of the 37,567 processed ISOT articles (99.6%) appear verbatim in WELFake (37,408 WELFake rows), so the full-corpus vocabulary overlap is near-total (ISOT OOV 0.00%, log-odds r = 0.91); against the 23,391 WELFake-only rows ({'real': 13533, 'fake': 9858}) the overlap is still high (Jaccard 75% top-5k / 47% all words, ISOT OOV 0.44%) and class cues transfer partially (r = 0.27, 55% same sign, 72% of the 50 most fake-leaning WELFake-only words also lean fake in ISOT). | A naive WELFake→ISOT 'cross-dataset' test would be an in-domain test in disguise → the cross-dataset protocol must train on **WELFake minus the ISOT articles** (hash-matched, plus MinHash near-duplicates) and test on ISOT (and the reverse), and the master doc §10/§18 wording 'ISOT for cross-dataset' must say so; the generalisation number the paper should trust most is the 2026 Live Claims Set, which is why evidence retrieval, not the classifier, decides the verdict. |
