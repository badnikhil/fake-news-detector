      Technical details

      About this tool

      What the verdicts mean

      ✔ REAL
      At least two reliable sources support the main claim and nothing important is contradicted.
      ✖ FAKE
      A fact-checker rates a matching claim false, or at least two reliable sources contradict it (or the input comes from a satire / known-hoax site).
      ◐ PARTIALLY TRUE
      The main claim is supported but another important claim is contradicted, or the important claims are split.
      ⚠ MISLEADING
      The facts are real but presented wrongly: old news shown as new, the wrong place or year, or a headline its own article contradicts.
      ? UNVERIFIABLE
      We could not find enough reliable evidence either way. This does not mean the claim is false.

      How it works

      Input → claim extraction (spaCy) → evidence retrieval (offline FAISS index of PolitiFact fact-checks; online: DuckDuckGo, Wikipedia, Google Fact Check) → stance detection (NLI, DeBERTa-v3-small) → date / context checks → rule-based fusion (rules R0–R9). The writing-style classifier only adjusts confidence by ±0.10 and breaks single-source ties; it never decides the verdict on its own.

        Limitations

        • English only. Other languages return UNVERIFIABLE.
        • Offline mode only knows the bundled PolitiFact ratings (LIAR + FakeNewsNet, up to about 2018); recent events come back UNVERIFIABLE.
        • Fact-check ratings are applied only when the claim restates the checked statement; paraphrases and negations can be missed.
        • Stance detection is imperfect (about 0.73 accuracy on a FEVER sample), so read the evidence cards, not just the label.
        • Style signals reflect training-data writing style, not truth.
